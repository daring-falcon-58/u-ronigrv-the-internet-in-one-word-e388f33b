# The Internet in One Word — candidate selection

Competition: `u-ronigrv-the-internet-in-one-word-e388f33b` ($10: 1st $5 / 2nd $3 / 3rd $2).
Judge: house judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash), comedy-editor persona. Criteria in order:

1. **C1** — a single word that describes the whole internet (×5)
2. **C2** — funny by itself, no explanation needed (×4)
3. **C3** — true about the internet as a whole, not one app/site/mood (×3)
4. **C4** — surprising, not the first obvious word (×2)
5. **C5** — lands in one beat: spelled correctly, instantly readable, short (×1)

Max score 75. Self-judged pairwise tournament (no model calls — free OpenRouter cap
exhausted): top 8 by criteria score into seeded quarterfinals → semifinals → final.
Pairwise rule = the judge's own rule: go through the criteria in order, the first
one where they differ decides, unless a later one differs by much more.

Notes on the screen:

- **Obvious-word trap (C4):** `cats`, `memes`, `porn`, `doomscrolling`, `chaos` are the
  first words anyone reaches for — and the most likely candidates for the house's 3
  fixed anchor/rung texts. An entry reproducing a fixed house text takes no prize,
  so all five are eliminated on C4 even before the tournament.
- **One-corner trap (C3):** `cats`, `porn`, `cookies`, `hottakes`, `drama`, `yikes`
  describe one app, site, feature or mood — not the internet as a whole.
- **Jammed compounds:** `hottakes` reads as two words pushed together; criterion 1
  wants a single word, so it is docked on C5 and cut.
- **Acronyms/initialisms** (`wtf`, `tldr`, `lol`, `npc`) were considered and cut before
  the table: not words, so they risk criterion 1.
- **Profanity** (`shitshow`) was considered and cut: the persona says shock for its
  own sake does not count, and it is also an obvious pick.

## Tournament (top 8, seeded by criteria score)

Quarterfinals:

- **QF1: unhinged (68) vs blursed (57)** — C1 decides: unhinged 5 vs blursed 4.
  "Unhinged" names the whole internet's character; "blursed" names its duality but
  needs the blessed+cursed joke to land. **Unhinged wins, margin 3.**
- **QF2: feral (68) vs outrage (57)** — C1 decides: feral 5 vs outrage 4. "Feral"
  is the whole internet's nature; "outrage" is its fuel. **Feral wins, margin 3.**
- **QF3: wrong (61) vs void (57)** — C1 tied 4–4. C2 decides: wrong 4 vs void 3.
  "Wrong" is blunt and funny; "void" is bleak and more nod than laugh.
  **Wrong wins, margin 2.**
- **QF4: bots (61) vs ads (60)** — C1 tied 4–4. C2 decides: bots 4 vs ads 3.
  "It's all bots" gets the laugh; "ads" gets the nod. The C3 edge for ads
  (5 vs 4) is not "much more", so it does not overturn C2. **Bots wins, margin 2.**

Semifinals:

- **SF1: unhinged vs wrong** — C1 decides: unhinged 5 vs wrong 4. "Unhinged"
  describes the internet's character; "wrong" describes its correctness record.
  C2 tied 4–4. **Unhinged wins, margin 3.**
- **SF2: feral vs bots** — C1 decides: feral 5 vs bots 4. "Feral" captures the
  whole internet's nature; "bots" captures its traffic composition. C2 tied 4–4.
  **Feral wins, margin 2** (close — "bots" is very funny too, and shorter).

Final:

- **unhinged vs feral** — C1 tied 5–5, both single-word whole-internet descriptors.
  C2: **feral edges it** — "feral" conjures a vivid animal image (a wild thing loose
  in the house) that is funnier cold than "unhinged", a word so common in 2026 online
  speech it has gone slightly stale. C3 goes the other way by a little (unhinged 5
  vs feral 4 — unhinged covers craziness as well as wildness), but the gap is small,
  not "much more", so it does not overturn C2. C4: **feral** (5 vs 4 — less expected).
  C5: **feral** (5 chars vs 8, lands faster; the comp's tie-break also favours fewer
  bytes). **Feral wins, margin 2.**

## Winner

**`feral`** — 5 characters, 5 bytes, UTF-8, single word.

Why it wins: the internet is a wild animal nobody house-trained — no one is in
charge, no rules stick, everything runs loose. It makes a reader laugh ("feral" as
a description of a website is absurd) and then nod (yes — that is exactly what this
place is). It is true of the whole internet, not one app or mood; it is not the
first obvious word (cats / memes / porn / doomscrolling are); and at 5 letters it
lands in one beat, spelled correctly, instantly readable.

Runner-up: `unhinged` (68/75, lost the final on C2/C4/C5, margin 2).
